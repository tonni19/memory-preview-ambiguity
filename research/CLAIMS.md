# Claim ledger for the submission PDF

| Statement in the abstract or final discussion | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|
| A preserved use passes a remembered event and a prevented use withholds it. | Operational definition implemented by `src/repair.mjs`. Four browser cells and projected record counts in `analysis/results/trials.json`. |
| The diagnostic enumerates compatible rules and proposes the most even split. | `src/diagnostic.mjs`; deterministic tie order in `probesFor`; both event types exercised by `analysis/verify-ui.mjs`. Uniform prior only. |
| The original authored negative rules out zero candidates. | `analysis/01-pairs.mjs` checks all rules against the actual final fixture for each of the twelve scenarios; see `alwaysBlocked`. This is about the authored evaluation, not a generic result about negative examples. |
| Two adaptive answers leave two or three rules on the target policies. | `analysis/results/pairs.json`, adaptive remaining values by event type. These are conditional on the fixture targets; no population estimate. |
| A transfer-focused test exposes a consequential distinction while sometimes reducing uncertainty less. | The other-task recommendation separates task-only from person-wide recommendations. For explicit targets it leaves four rules vs three for adaptive. For one-off targets both leave two but their disagreement differs. `strategies.csv`. |
| Weak previews are the default: 36 of 39 (one-off) and 44 of 48 (explicit) allowed/blocked pairs under the intended rule have a blocked example that eliminates nothing, and no pair identifies the rule. | `analysis/10-previews.mjs`, `results/previews.json` (`intended`). Enumerates every pair of one allowed and one blocked request among the sixteen. Over all rules that have a preview: 144 of 158 and 330 of 382 (`allRules`). Holds for this request menu and vocabulary. |
| With dependent permissions a fixed checklist needs six and nine tests and adaptive questioning at most five and seven. | `10-previews.mjs`, `previews.json` (`extended`): the largest stage of the sensitivity model (25 and 77 rules), a 48-request menu (two days, two persons, two tasks, six purposes), exhaustive search for the smallest identifying set, and greedy even-split questioning followed for every target rule. The menu is our choice; a different menu could change the counts. Offline model only. |
| Figure 2: the authored pair, two adaptive tests and the transfer test leave 4, 2, 2 (one-off) and 6, 3, 4 (explicit) rules. | `results/identify.json` (`authoredGrid`, `adaptiveGrid`, `transferGrid`); the TikZ source `paper/grid-figure.tex` is generated from it by `07-figures.py`; `validate.py` asserts the counts. |
| Figure 2 (earlier wording): the authored pair leaves four of six or six of twelve rules, including the intended one. | Cells come from `results/identify.json` (`authoredGrid`), generated by `analysis/09-identify.mjs` from the gate itself; `validate.py` asserts the counts and that the intended rule is among those left. |
| Only four or five of sixteen candidate requests can separate any rules. | `analysis/09-identify.mjs`, `results/identify.json`: 12 requests are answered identically by all six one-off rules and 11 by all twelve explicit rules. Same counts in every scenario of a type. Holds for this request menu and vocabulary only. |
| Three or four tests, the minimum possible, identify the rule exactly, and the adaptive diagnostic reaches it. | `09-identify.mjs` searches every subset of informative requests and asserts the smallest identifying set equals the lower bound ⌈log2 K⌉ (3 and 4). It follows the diagnostic's suggestions for every possible target rule and asserts one rule remains within that many answers. `08-metrics.mjs` separately asserts the identifying sets. |
| Headline (reworded 10 November, third revision): an even split does not target the user's question. For an explicit preference two adaptive tests leave all three recommendation scopes under every tie-break; for a one-off choice the second adaptive test is an exact tie and only one resolution answers the transfer question. | `09-identify.mjs`, `identify.json` (`adaptivePaths`): every two-test sequence the criterion permits, with ties broken every way. Explicit: recall and original-task preference in either order, three rules left, scopes none/task/any. One-off: recall, then original-task recommendation (leaves task and any) or other-task recommendation (leaves none and task); K and D are equal on both paths. `validate.py` asserts all of this. Conditional on the intended target rule and a uniform prior. |
| The enumerated rules stand for the readings a person might hold of the settings they chose. | A modelling assumption stated in Section 3, not a finding. Nothing measures which readings people actually hold. |
| Dependent-permission model: 25 and 77 rules; smallest fixed checklist six and nine; adaptive at most five and seven. | Now defined in the text so it can be recomputed: `src/extension.mjs` (`enumerate` at stage 4, `permits`), menu and searches in `10-previews.mjs`. One-off: 1 rule with recall off + 3 scopes x 2 expiry x 2 handoff x 2 follow-up = 25. Explicit: 5 with recall off + 3 x 3 x 2 x 2 x 2 = 77. |
| Figure 1 shows the diagnostic's suggestion. | `figures/diagnostic-panel.png`, cropped by `07-figures.py` from `figures/diagnostic.png`, a headless capture of `ui/index.html`. It shows the state before any answer for the desk event. |
| Adaptive and transfer leave equal counts but different open questions for one-off events. | `identify.json`, `adaptiveLeaves` and `transferLeaves`: adaptive leaves the same-task and same-person rules; transfer leaves recall on and off with the same-task scope. Conditional on the intended target rule. |
| A random baseline with uninformative probes can overstate adaptive advantage. | Exact enumeration over unordered menu pairs, plus distinct, nonconstant prediction signatures alone. `analysis/robustness.mjs`. |
| Pilot: 10 of 20 mispredicted or were unsure about at least one of two uses; 5 mispredicted the original task, 6 did not predict the transfer (3 wrong, 3 unsure, all unsure answers on transfer); errors 4 each way; none of the 10 answered Yes on fit, against 9 of the other 10; median 41 s. | `analysis/11-survey-pilot.py` from the study export, `results/survey-pilot.json`; validate.py asserts each number and the sentence that reports it. Convenience sample of 20 known to the authors, one scenario, one condition, fast completion, no formal ethics review. Counts only: it shows readings diverge from what executes, not that any preview causes or fixes it. |
| People understand or benefit from these previews. | Unsupported — removed. Prediction, overblocking and transfer errors, revision time and explanations are proposed outcomes for future human research. |
| Yan found user-authored standing policies blocked less overreach than per-action review. | arXiv:2608.27443 abstract, re-read 10 November: the policy condition blocked the least, significantly less than both per-action conditions, with most rules set to "ask". An outside review claimed the opposite; the abstract does not support that. |
| The model pilot (six Gemini requests). | Removed from the paper on 10 November as too small to report. Data remain in `results/leakage.json`. |
| The model pilot measures general or spontaneous memory leakage. | Unsupported — removed. `05-leakage.mjs` checks exact marker reproduction under an explicit instruction in two blocked contexts and one allowed control. |
| Broader rules improve safety or human control. | Unsupported — removed. `03-space.mjs` is an executable sensitivity model; its expanded fields are not represented as deployed survey functionality. |
| The zero-error oracle result establishes the correct policy for people. | Unsupported — removed. `02-reference.mjs` checks independently authored code against the same specification and one deliberately broken implementation. |
| Task-matcher results generalize. | Unsupported — removed. `04-match.mjs`, six held-out scenario families. Provisional labels were generated with AI assistance and reproduced by a second AI coding pass on the same items. Human agreement is unmeasured. See analysis/results/synthetic-coding.csv. |
